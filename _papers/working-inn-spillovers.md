When startups innovate, does the surrounding area prosper? Exploiting quasi-random assignment of patent examiners with varying leniency, we show that patent grants to startups increase nearby annualized real estate returns by 0.4 to 0.6 percentage points for residential properties and 0.5 to 0.9 percentage points for commercial properties and vacant land. Effects are largest for the first successful startup in an area and decline with additional successes. We also find that patent-granted startups spur nearby patenting, establishments, and jobs. Yet these real effects emerge only over the following decade, while real estate return premia appear immediately.
