U.S. homebuilders often respond to rising mortgage rates by offering below-market financing through lenders they own. Using linked loan, transaction, and lender-ownership data from the fifty largest metropolitan areas, we study how much these rate discounts lower buyer costs, who receives them, and whether they expand mortgage access at the cost of additional credit risk. After 2021, rate spreads fall by 0.43 percentage points at lenders wholly owned by the selling builder and by 0.22 at joint ventures, relative to unaffiliated lenders. Builders that own lenders cut both prices and rates rather than raising home prices to offset lower rates, and they offer larger discounts where competition for buyers is stronger. Borrowers at builder-affiliated lenders shift toward relatively lower incomes, but we find no evidence that these lenders relax underwriting or originate loans with higher early delinquency.
